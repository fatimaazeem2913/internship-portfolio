==========================================================================================
POLYSEMY DEMONSTRATION: static embeddings cannot distinguish word senses
==========================================================================================

Word under test: "light"

Sentence A (idiomatic sense - "reveal/inform"):
  "They shed light on social and economic issues, including student debt and food insecurity"

Sentence B (literal sense - "electromagnetic radiation"):
  "A Sun-like star is about a billion times brighter than the reflected light from any exoplanet orbiting it"

Word2Vec vector for 'light' when it appears in Sentence A (first 10 dims):
  [ 0.11296692 -0.28799245 -0.6983724   0.14174198  0.65472406 -0.4072319
  0.0280142   0.4665868   0.08438891 -0.68175375]

Word2Vec vector for 'light' when it appears in Sentence B (first 10 dims):
  [ 0.11296692 -0.28799245 -0.6983724   0.14174198  0.65472406 -0.4072319
  0.0280142   0.4665868   0.08438891 -0.68175375]

Are the two vectors IDENTICAL? True  <- there is only ONE vector for 'light' in the whole model
Cosine similarity between 'the vector' and 'itself': 1.000000

CONCLUSION:
Word2Vec (and any static embedding table) stores exactly ONE vector per
word STRING, learned once during training by averaging over every context
that word ever appeared in across the whole corpus. It has no mechanism to
look at the CURRENT sentence and adjust the vector on the fly. So whether
'light' means 'illumination/photons' or 'to reveal/inform,' the model hands
back the exact same 100-dimensional number every single time, because from
the model's point of view, it's just one lookup-table row indexed by the
word string -- context never enters the lookup at inference time.
