PROBLEM 1: A bat and a ball cost $1.10 in total. The bat costs $1.00 more than the ball. How much does the ball cost?
GROUND TRUTH: $0.05

  [DIRECT PROMPT: "What is the answer? Answer immediately."]
  Answer: $0.10   INCORRECT
  (Why this happens: Fast pattern-match: $1.10 total, bat costs $1 more, so ball = $0.10 'feels' right at a glance.)

  [CoT PROMPT: "Let's think step by step."]
  Reasoning: Let ball = x. Then bat = x + 1.00. Total: x + (x + 1.00) = 1.10 => 2x + 1.00 = 1.10 => 2x = 0.10 => x = 0.05. Check: ball=$0.05, bat=$1.05, total=$1.10 (correct), bat is $1.00 more than ball (correct).
  Answer: $0.05   CORRECT

PROBLEM 2: If it takes 5 machines 5 minutes to make 5 widgets, how long would it take 100 machines to make 100 widgets?
GROUND TRUTH: 5 minutes

  [DIRECT PROMPT: "What is the answer? Answer immediately."]
  Answer: 100 minutes   INCORRECT
  (Why this happens: Fast pattern-match: scaling widgets 5->100 (20x) 'feels' like it should scale time too.)

  [CoT PROMPT: "Let's think step by step."]
  Reasoning: 5 machines make 5 widgets in 5 minutes => each machine makes 1 widget per 5 minutes (rate is per-machine, independent of how many machines run in parallel). 100 machines running in parallel each make 1 widget in the same 5 minutes => 100 machines make 100 widgets in 5 minutes.
  Answer: 5 minutes   CORRECT

PROBLEM 3: A patch of lily pads on a lake doubles in size every day. If it takes 48 days for the patch to cover the entire lake, how many days would it take to cover HALF the lake?
GROUND TRUTH: 47 days

  [DIRECT PROMPT: "What is the answer? Answer immediately."]
  Answer: 24 days   INCORRECT
  (Why this happens: Fast pattern-match: half the coverage 'feels' like it should take half the time (48/2=24).)

  [CoT PROMPT: "Let's think step by step."]
  Reasoning: The patch DOUBLES each day. If it fully covers the lake on day 48, then on day 47 it must have covered exactly half (since doubling half gives the full lake one day later). So half coverage happens at day 47, not day 24.
  Answer: 47 days   CORRECT

PROBLEM 4: Sarah has 3 boxes of apples with 12 apples each. She gives away 8 apples, then buys 2 more boxes of 12 apples each. How many apples does she have now?
GROUND TRUTH: 52

  [DIRECT PROMPT: "What is the answer? Answer immediately."]
  Answer: 30   INCORRECT
  (Why this happens: Fast mental math slip: 3x12=36, minus 8 = 28, then '+2 more boxes' misread as '+2' instead of '+2x12=24', giving 28+2=30.)

  [CoT PROMPT: "Let's think step by step."]
  Reasoning: Step 1: 3 boxes x 12 apples = 36 apples. Step 2: gives away 8 -> 36 - 8 = 28 apples. Step 3: buys 2 MORE BOXES of 12 each = 2 x 12 = 24 apples. Step 4: 28 + 24 = 52 apples total.
  Answer: 52   CORRECT

PROBLEM 5: A shirt costs $80. It is on sale for 25% off. At checkout there is an ADDITIONAL 10% off the already-discounted price. What is the final price?
GROUND TRUTH: $54.00

  [DIRECT PROMPT: "What is the answer? Answer immediately."]
  Answer: $52.00   INCORRECT
  (Why this happens: Fast pattern-match: adds the two percentages together (25%+10%=35% off), then computes 80 x (1 - 0.35) = 80 x 0.65 = $52 -- treats sequential discounts as if they were additive.)

  [CoT PROMPT: "Let's think step by step."]
  Reasoning: Step 1: first discount -- 80 x (1 - 0.25) = 80 x 0.75 = $60. Step 2: second discount applies to the NEW price of $60, not the original $80 -- 60 x (1 - 0.10) = 60 x 0.90 = $54. Sequential percentage discounts multiply, they don't add.
  Answer: $54.00   CORRECT

PROBLEM 6: If 3 cats can catch 3 mice in 3 minutes, how many mice can 100 cats catch in 100 minutes?
GROUND TRUTH: approximately 3333 mice

  [DIRECT PROMPT: "What is the answer? Answer immediately."]
  Answer: 100 mice   INCORRECT
  (Why this happens: Fast pattern-match: mirrors the 'widgets' problem's misleading intuition -- assumes the answer just equals the number of cats (or of minutes), landing on 100 as a 'clean' looking number.)

  [CoT PROMPT: "Let's think step by step."]
  Reasoning: 3 cats catch 3 mice in 3 minutes => rate = 1 mouse per cat per 3 minutes. In 100 minutes, one cat catches 100/3 ~= 33.33 mice. 100 cats (running in parallel) each catch 33.33 mice => 100 x 33.33 ~= 3333.33 mice total.
  Answer: approximately 3333 mice   CORRECT

PROBLEM 7: All squares are rectangles. Some rectangles are not squares. Is the statement 'no rectangles are squares' TRUE or FALSE?
GROUND TRUTH: FALSE

  [DIRECT PROMPT: "What is the answer? Answer immediately."]
  Answer: TRUE   INCORRECT
  (Why this happens: Fast pattern-match: 'some rectangles are not squares' gets carelessly conflated with 'no rectangles are squares' -- confusing 'some...not' with 'none'.)

  [CoT PROMPT: "Let's think step by step."]
  Reasoning: Premise 1: all squares are rectangles (squares are a SUBSET of rectangles). Premise 2: some rectangles are not squares (the subset is proper, not everything). Since squares ARE rectangles (premise 1), and squares clearly exist, it is false that 'no rectangles are squares' -- some rectangles (the squares) definitely ARE squares. The statement contradicts premise 1 directly.
  Answer: FALSE   CORRECT

PROBLEM 8: Tom has 5 red marbles and 7 blue marbles. He is 10 years old. He gives 3 red marbles to his sister. How many marbles does Tom have left?
GROUND TRUTH: 9

  [DIRECT PROMPT: "What is the answer? Answer immediately."]
  Answer: 9   CORRECT
  (Why this happens: This is a CONTROL question -- simple arithmetic with an irrelevant distractor (age). Both direct and careful reasoning should get this right, showing CoT isn't universally necessary for EVERY problem.)

  [CoT PROMPT: "Let's think step by step."]
  Reasoning: 5 + 7 = 12 total marbles. Age (10) is irrelevant distractor information. Gives away 3 red: 12 - 3 = 9 marbles remaining.
  Answer: 9   CORRECT

OBSERVATIONS:
- All 6 of the DIRECT-prompt errors are not random -- they are well-documented,
  systematic 'tempting fast answer' patterns (the bat-and-ball problem is a
  classic Cognitive Reflection Test item specifically because most people,
  and apparently many LLM responses without explicit reasoning, fall for the
  same $0.10 trap).
- CoT did not help problem 8 because it didn't NEED to -- it's a control
  question with no multi-step arithmetic trap, included specifically to show
  CoT is not universally necessary; its benefit is concentrated on genuinely
  multi-step or counter-intuitive problems.
- The pattern across problems 1, 3, 5, 6, 7 is consistent: DIRECT answers fail
  specifically when the problem has a superficially-similar-looking but
  mathematically WRONG shortcut available (adding percentages, halving time
  linearly, conflating 'some not' with 'none'). CoT's benefit comes precisely
  from forcing the intermediate steps that expose why the shortcut is wrong.
