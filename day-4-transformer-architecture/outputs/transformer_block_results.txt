Per-token std devs after:   [1. 1. 1.]   <- all ~1

Critically, normalization happens PER TOKEN across features. Token 3's
wildly different scale (-5 to 100) is normalized using only token 3's own
statistics -- it never looks at the other tokens or other batch examples.
This is what makes LayerNorm batch-size-independent and safe at inference.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
COMPONENT: Residual Connections
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The residual pattern is: output = x + Sublayer(x)

Why this matters -- gradient flow. Consider the derivative:
    d(x + F(x))/dx = 1 + dF(x)/dx

That '+1' term is the key. Even if dF/dx is tiny (a sublayer that barely
affects its input), the gradient still flows backward at full strength
through the identity path. Stacking 96 layers is only feasible because
each residual connection guarantees an unobstructed gradient highway.

Empirical check -- simulating stacked sublayers whose local derivative
dF/dx = 0.7 (a plausibly 'weak' sublayer):

  Layers    Without residual      With residual         
  ----------------------------------------------------
  10        2.825e-02             2.016e+02             
  25        1.341e-04             5.771e+05             
  50        1.798e-08             3.330e+11             
  96        1.347e-15             1.328e+22             

  Without residuals, the gradient decays exponentially toward zero.
  With residuals, the '+1' identity term means the gradient does NOT
  decay -- it actually grows, which is why Transformers additionally
  need LayerNorm to keep the magnitudes controlled. The two components
  work as a pair: residuals guarantee gradient flow, LayerNorm keeps
  the resulting magnitudes numerically stable.

(Compare this to the Day 3 LSTM result, where the gradient literally hit
 0.000000 across just 55 timesteps -- same underlying math, and residual
 connections are precisely the architectural fix.)

This confirms the FFN does NO cross-token mixing. All communication
between positions happens exclusively in the attention sublayer -- the
FFN just processes each token's representation on its own.
